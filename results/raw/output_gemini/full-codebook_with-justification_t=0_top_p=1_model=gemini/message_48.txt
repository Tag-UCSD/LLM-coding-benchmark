User: Du Bois was born in Massachusetts in 1868. He gained initial prominence in 1903 with a book, "The Souls of Black Folks," that said blacks should be more aggressive in opposing the segregationist laws that had replaced the laws governing slaves. In 1909, Du Bois helped to found the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People and for 24 years edited its magazine, Crisis. In the 1930's and 40's, Du Bois drifted away from the N.A.A.C.P., saying it had come to reflect middle-class values and was not helping the poorest blacks. In the 50's, Du Bois became increasingly critical of the United States, and in 1961, at the age of 93, he joined the Communist Party and moved to Ghana, where he died two years later. In response to criticism that the Postal Service should not honor a Communist, Donald McDowell, who heads the philatelic program, said in an interview with Linn's Stamp News, "There was much more to Du Bois than that bitter chapter of his life." Collectors who want a first-day cancellation of the Du Bois stamp should buy the stamp at a post office and affix it to an addressed envelope. The envelope, of course, can be decorated in any way, which teachers might use as a tool to teach history to younger children. The envelopes should be sent in a larger envelope to Du Bois Stamp, c/o Postmaster, 3900 Crown Road, Atlanta, Ga. 30304. There is no charge for the service. New From the U.N.
