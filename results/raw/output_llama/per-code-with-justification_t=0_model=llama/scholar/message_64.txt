User: In April, Chavis, who is 46, completed his first year as executive director of the Baltimore-based N.A.A.C.P., and it is fair to say that in that short time Chavis has not punked out. He has placed himself at the epicenter of a vigorous, heated debate over what direction the once-vital civil rights movement should take. In the simplest of terms, the debate has pitted longtime integrationists against a growing black nationalist sentiment. It is what one historian calls "a timeless conundrum" that ripens with each new generation of black leaders. W. E .B. Du Bois debated Booker T. Washington on just that subject; the Rev. Martin Luther King Jr. tangled with Malcolm X.
